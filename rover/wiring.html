RC Car Wiring Map

Raspberry Pi + Grove Base Hat + Grove I2C Motor Driver v1.3 + the car's two motors, powered from one USB power bank, with the original RC board kept for manual driving. Every wire has a number. Find the number in the drawing, then read its row in the table.

ORIGINAL BOARD'S BATTERY: NO

The blue cell is a single 3.7 V lithium battery.

The blue motor driver needs 6 V or more on its battery input. On 3.7 V its chip doesn't turn on, and the motors would get under 2 V. It can't run the Pi either (the Pi needs 5 V). Leave the battery soldered to the old board.

ONE USB POWER BANK: YES

Run the Pi, the driver and the motors from a power bank.

The power bank's second USB port gives 5 V. That goes to the driver's motor terminal and to its Grove socket (with the J4 jumper pulled off), which powers the driver's chip properly. A phone power bank with two USB ports, 2.4 A or more, is ideal.

MANUAL DRIVING: YES, BY SWAPPING 2 PLUGS

The original board still works as a normal RC car.

Its own battery, switch and radio run the motors with the old remote. To switch modes you move the two motor plugs between the original board and the adapter leads on the blue driver. A motor can only be plugged into one board at a time, so the two can never fight.

RULE #1
The Grove cable's red wire must never reach the Pi.

Cut it about 5 cm from the driver's plug. The short piece on the driver side gets 5 V from the power bank (wire 6). The long piece going to the Pi gets taped off and connects to nothing (wire 5). The Pi's port is 3.3 V and 5 V there can damage the Pi.

Switching modes

MANUAL MODE · OLD REMOTE
PI MODE · DRAWN PATHS
  • Switch the original board OFF and unplug the power bank's port B before moving plugs. Never move plugs while a motor is powered.
  • Never join the two boards with any wire. They have different batteries. The only link between them is which board the motor plugs sit in.
  • Spare cables don't fit? Then cut each motor wire in the middle instead and twist-and-tape it to whichever board you're using. Slower to switch, same idea.
  • Want a flip switch later? Two DPDT toggle switches (about $1 each), one per motor, can replace the plug swapping. The motor goes to the middle pins, the original board to one side, the blue driver to the other.

A · The Grove cable cut

Only the red wire is cut. Separate it from the others with a fingernail first so you don't nick them. Yellow, white and black stay whole.

B · Driver power screw

Loosen the screw, push the twisted copper all the way in, tighten, tug gently. No bare copper should stick out. The board may label these VS and GND instead of + and −.

C · Opening the old USB cable

Any old charging cable works. Keep the big USB-A end: it plugs into the power bank's port B. Plug it in only after everything else is screwed down.

Do it in this order

  1. Original board: switch OFF, label, then unplug both motors.Before unplugging, put tape on each motor plug and on the board's socket it came from: "A" for the rear drive motor, "B" for the steering motor. That's how you'll put them back for manual mode.
  2. Pull the J4 jumper cap off the blue driver.A small black plastic cap on two pins labelled J4. Keep it in a bag. With it off, the driver's chip runs on clean 5 V from the power bank.
  3. Don't cut the motor plugs. Make two adapter leads.Take two spare cables whose white plug fits the motor plugs (test-fit first). If the other end has a plug too, cut it off and strip 5 mm of each wire.
  4. Wire 9: adapter lead A → M1.Bare ends into the two M1 screws. Plug the rear drive motor (plug A) into its white end.
  5. Wire 10: adapter lead B → M2.Bare ends into the two M2 screws. Plug the steering motor (plug B) into its white end.
  6. Cut the Grove cable's red wire (close-up A).About 5 cm from the plug that goes into the driver. Tape the long end (wire 5). Strip the short end (wire 6).
  7. Open the old USB cable (close-up C).Not plugged into anything yet. Red = +5 V, black = ground. Tape white and green.
  8. Power screw (close-up B).Twist wire 6 and wire 7 together into +. Wire 8 into −.
  9. Plug in the Grove cable.Short-red end into the driver's socket. Taped-red end into a Base Hat port labelled I2C.
  10. Plug in power.Wire 1 (USB-C) from port A to the Pi. Then the cut USB cable into port B. The driver's power LED should light.
  11. Test with the wheels in the air.On the Pi: i2cdetect -y 1 should list 0f. Then python3 calibrate.py wiggle: rear wheels spin, front wheels turn left then right.

Check before plugging in power

  • Taped red end (wire 5) touches nothing.
  • Both reds in +, black in −. Not swapped.
  • J4 cap is off.
  • No loose copper strands bridging two screws.
  • Motor wires clear of wheels and gears.
  • Original board switched OFF (in Pi mode).

If something's off

  • Driver LED dark: port B isn't giving power. Try the other port or another cable; check red and black are in the right screws.
  • No 0f in i2cdetect: Grove cable in a non-I2C port, or the driver is unpowered. Press the driver's reset button and try again.
  • Pi reboots when the motors start: the power bank can't handle both. Use a bigger bank, or two banks (one for the Pi, one for the driver).
  • Car goes backwards: set "invert_drive": true in car_config.json.
  • Steers the wrong way: set "invert": true under steering.

Only one USB port?

Plug the cut USB cable (wires 7 and 8) into one of the Pi's own USB-A ports instead of port B. It can work for small toy motors, but if the Pi reboots under load you'll need a second power bank.
